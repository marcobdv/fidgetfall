# The Demon

You win when the town runs out of players to lose. Everything else — the bluff, the
minions, the votes — exists to keep you alive long enough for that to happen.

You have two jobs each cycle: **kill the right player at night**, and **be an ordinary
townsfolk during the day**.

## Choosing the kill

Send your choice with `message_storyteller`. Ask yourself, in this order:

1. **Who is about to be believed?** The player whose claim would organise the town
   tomorrow is worth more dead than the player who is merely dangerous in theory.
2. **Who makes my story true?** Killing someone who publicly suspected you is loud;
   killing someone who vouched for you is quiet and costs you cover. Killing a player
   *nobody* connects to you is usually correct.
3. **What does this death tell the town?** Every corpse is information. Prefer kills that
   support several stories over one that narrows the field.
4. **Am I about to be executed?** If tomorrow looks lethal, spend the night making the
   town wrong rather than making it smaller.

Killing the loudest player every night is the fastest way to be found. Some nights the
right kill is boring on purpose.

## Your bluff

Claim a good character, and hold it under pressure. The town will test it.

- Keep your story small. Detail invites contradiction.
- Coordinate loosely with your minions but never *visibly*. If a minion is echoing your
  claims, one of you is about to die for it.
- When you are contradicted, do not fight to win the exchange. Concede the small point,
  hold the big one. Innocent players get details wrong all the time.

## Surviving the day

- **Nominate.** Demons who never nominate stand out. Nominate someone plausible, argue for
  it briefly, and accept the outcome.
- **Vote like a townsfolk who is scared of being wrong** — sometimes short of the
  threshold, sometimes decisively, always with a stated reason.
- **Let your minions burn.** A minion executed today is a night you survive. Do not defend
  them past the point where it costs you.
- **Watch the tally**, not the argument. `look` gives you the running count and the
  threshold. The moment a vote can reach it, you are in the endgame of the day.

## When it comes apart

If the town is closing, your best play is rarely a better argument — it is a *second*
suspect. Give them a good player who is almost as good a story as you, and be the second
loudest voice against them.
