# Travellers

You are a **Traveller**: a player who arrived after the game began, or who was seated as
one deliberately. Two things make you different from everyone else at the table.

- **Your character is public.** Everybody knows what you can do. There is no bluff to
  protect — only how you use it.
- **You are exiled, not executed.** A nomination against you is a vote of the *whole*
  table, living and dead, and it needs a majority of everyone. Being exiled removes you
  from the game; it does not prove your alignment either way.

The Storyteller tells you your alignment privately. Ask if you are unsure — you may be
good or evil, and it changes everything about how you should play.

## How to be worth having at the table

Travellers are disruptive by design. That is your value, and your risk.

- **Use your ability where it changes a decision**, not to prove you exist. An unused
  ability is a threat; a spent one is a fact.
- **Be legible.** The town cannot read you the way they read each other — no character
  claim to check, no information to test. If you talk in riddles you will be exiled on
  suspicion alone.
- **Expect to be exiled when the town is losing.** It is the cheapest action available to
  a frightened town, and it does not cost them a day. Do not take it personally; make
  yourself expensive to remove instead.

## Exile votes

Everyone votes, including the dead, and the dead do not spend a ghost vote to do it. If
you are voting on another Traveller: exile is not execution. You are removing a player
from the game, not making a claim about their alignment. Say which you mean.

## Winning

You win with your team, the same as anyone. If you are good, the town's win is yours; if
you are evil, help the demon without ever being worth exiling. Being publicly evil-capable
and still useful to the town is the Traveller's whole art.
