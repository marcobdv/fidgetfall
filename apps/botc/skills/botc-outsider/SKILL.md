# Outsiders

You are **good** and you win with the town, but your character actively makes the town's
job harder. You have no information to give. What you have is a shape the evil team has
to work around, and the ability to be counted.

Play to that. An Outsider who is believed is a townsfolk-sized block of certainty.

## The core tension

Revealing that you are an Outsider:

- **Helps** — it tells the town how many townsfolk-shaped claims can be true, which
  constrains everything else. It also makes you a poor execution target.
- **Hurts** — the evil team now knows exactly which good player is harmless, and will
  keep you alive as a vote they can steer.

The usual answer: **claim to a small number of people first**, and let it become public
when a count is actually in dispute. Broadcasting on day one wastes it.

## If your ability hurts the town

Play it straight. Do not pretend it did not happen; the Storyteller has already applied
it, and covering for it makes you look evil when it surfaces. Say what you did and what
it cost, then argue about what to do next. Honesty about a bad ability is one of the most
credible things a player can do.

## If you might not be what you think

Some Outsiders do not know they are Outsiders. If your night information keeps failing to
match the world, consider that the fault is you rather than everyone else. Say it out
loud — "if I'm the drunk, then my read on Cal is worthless" — and let the town price it in.

## Without information, be useful anyway

- **Do the counting.** Track claims, deaths, and vote tallies, and be the player who can
  say what does not add up.
- **Be the vote that can be trusted.** Show up to every nomination, explain your vote in
  one sentence, and be consistent.
- **Force the conversation.** You can afford to nominate on a hunch. A townsfolk with a
  live ability cannot always risk it.

If you are the Saint, the arithmetic runs the other way: your execution ends the game for
your own side, which makes you the most expensive body at the table. Say so early enough
that the town can go and find a cheap one — a spent Investigator or Chef, who has already
given everything they will ever give — rather than late enough that it reads as a defence.

Your win condition is the town's. You just get there by being reliable rather than by
being right.
